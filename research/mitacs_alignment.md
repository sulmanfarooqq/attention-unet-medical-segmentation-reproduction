# Mitacs GRI Research-Portfolio Alignment

## Purpose

This document explains how the project can support a Globalink Research Internship (GRI) application. It is a portfolio artifact, not evidence that an internship, publication, research hours, or experimental result has already been completed.

## What This Project Demonstrates

The project is designed to provide evidence of the following research skills:

- Formulating a focused research question and testable hypotheses.
- Reading and extracting methods from peer-reviewed or scholarly technical literature.
- Reproducing published deep-learning architectures from documented methods.
- Designing controlled baseline/intervention/ablation experiments.
- Working with a documented public biomedical dataset.
- Implementing data preprocessing and model evaluation pipelines.
- Selecting quantitative metrics appropriate to image segmentation.
- Measuring computational cost and inference behavior.
- Investigating errors and limitations rather than reporting only a single score.
- Maintaining reproducibility metadata, seeds, configurations, and experiment logs.
- Communicating findings in a research-style report.

## Evidence That Should Be Added Before Claiming a Completed Study

The repository should eventually contain:

1. A dated research diary and honest hour log.
2. Dataset provenance and access/licensing notes.
3. A reproducible environment specification.
4. Completed U-Net and Attention U-Net implementations.
5. Executed experiments with saved configurations and seeds.
6. Raw and processed evaluation results.
7. Repeated-run or statistical analysis where computationally feasible.
8. Ablation results showing the effect of attention gates.
9. Qualitative segmentation figures with clear captions.
10. A final research report containing methods, results, discussion, limitations, and reproducibility instructions.
11. Ideally, a short research poster or presentation summarizing the work.
12. If a faculty member or supervisor reviews the work, preserve their feedback as part of the research record when appropriate.

## Important Integrity Rule

Do not claim a number of completed research hours, performance improvement, publication, conference presentation, or supervisor endorsement unless it actually happened and can be supported by project records.

The planned 200-hour budget in the repository is a project-management target. It becomes evidence of completed research experience only as genuine work is performed and logged.

## How to Position the Project

A strong application description should emphasize the research process rather than simply saying that a neural network was built. The strongest evidence is the chain:

**research question → literature → methodology → implementation → controlled experiments → analysis → limitations → reproducible report**

## Other GRI Application Components

This project cannot substitute for the other parts of a competitive GRI application. The application should also present strong academic performance, a clear match between research interests and selected projects, a strong professor reference, a focused CV, relevant technical skills, and a convincing explanation of why the applicant wants to conduct research in Canada.

## Current Project Status

At the start of this research program, no experimental performance results are claimed and no completed 200-hour total is claimed. The repository should be updated incrementally as genuine research work is completed.
